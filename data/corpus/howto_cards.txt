HOWTO: cv_structure | Source: National Careers Service — CV sections | Tags: cv, application, prepare. Tip: Keep a CV to one page if you can. Use clear headings: contact details, personal profile (2–3 lines), education, experience, skills. Put the most relevant section near the top for this employer or course. Do: Write one short profile line that names the kind of role or course you want and one strength you can prove. Then list education and two experience bullets that match this match card. Do not claim: Do not claim a perfect CV guarantees an interview.

HOWTO: cv_experience | Source: National Careers Service — CV sections | Tags: cv, experience, skills. Tip: Experience can be paid work, volunteering, caring, school projects, clubs, or helping at home. Focus on what you did and what changed because of you. Do: For each experience line use: what you did + who for + one result or skill (for example served customers, kept a till accurate, or led a group task). Do not claim: Do not invent job titles or results you cannot explain if asked.

HOWTO: star_method | Source: Prospects — STAR technique | Tags: cv, interview, application, star. Tip: STAR means Situation, Task, Action, Result. It helps you turn a real example into a clear story for applications and interviews. Do: Pick one real challenge. Write four short lines: where you were, what you needed to do, what YOU did, and what happened next. Practise saying it in under two minutes. Do not claim: Do not claim STAR answers guarantee a job offer.

HOWTO: tailor_application | Source: Prospects — CVs and cover letters | Tags: cv, application, tailor. Tip: Strong applications mirror the skills the employer or course asks for. Generic CVs are easier to ignore. Do: From this match summary, list three skills or themes. Add one proof line from your profile for each (course, hobby, or experience). Do not claim: Do not invent skills you do not have.

HOWTO: check_before_apply | Source: National Careers Service — applications | Tags: apply, research, website. Tip: Before you apply, check the official page for how to apply, what they ask for, and whether applications are open. Do not guess closing dates. Do: Open the official website on your match card. Note the apply route and two things they care about. Save the page or email the link to yourself. Do not claim: Do not invent vacancies, fees, or deadlines.

HOWTO: find_openings | Source: GOV.UK — Find an apprenticeship | Tags: apprenticeship, vacancies, search. Tip: Live apprenticeship adverts sit on Find an apprenticeship and sometimes on the employer site. A match is not the same as an open vacancy. Do: Search the employer name and your town or postcode on Find an apprenticeship. Also check the employer careers page. Write down any live roles or “no vacancies”. Do not claim: Do not assume a role is open because the employer is a good match.

HOWTO: send_enquiry | Source: National Careers Service — contacting employers | Tags: enquiry, email, apply. Tip: A short enquiry works when you cannot apply yet. Keep it polite, specific, and under a few short paragraphs. Do: Draft: who you are, why this employer or course fits your interests, one question (how to apply / next open day / work experience policy), and thanks. Send only via an official contact route. Do not claim: Do not promise they will reply or offer you a place.

HOWTO: local_support | Source: Gatsby Good Career Guidance (applied) | Tags: adviser, support, plan. Tip: Talking your plan through with a careers person, teacher, tutor, or youth worker helps turn information into a next step. Do: Book or ask for a short chat. Take this match name, one interest from your profile, and two questions you still need answered. Do not claim: An app plan is not a replacement for personal guidance.

HOWTO: course_check | Source: UCAS / provider course pages (applied) | Tags: education, course, entry. Tip: Course pages list what you need, how to apply, and what the course covers. Always check the provider’s own page for current details. Do: On the official course page, note entry needs, start timing if shown, and the apply or enquiry button. Write two questions if anything is unclear. Do not claim: Do not invent grades, fees, or start dates.

HOWTO: personal_statement_prep | Source: UCAS — personal statement advice | Tags: education, personal_statement, prepare. Tip: Personal statements work best when they link your interests and experience to the course, with real examples rather than buzzwords. Do: Write three bullets: why this subject, one experience that shows interest, and what you want to learn. Keep language plain and honest. Do not claim: Do not copy statements from the internet.

HOWTO: interview_prep | Source: National Careers Service — interview advice | Tags: interview, prepare, star. Tip: Interviews go better when you know the organisation, can give one clear example, and have a question ready to ask them. Do: Read the match summary. Prepare one STAR example linked to your experience. Prepare one question about training or the first weeks in the role or course. Do not claim: Do not invent interview dates or formats.

HOWTO: military_official | Source: Official Armed Forces careers (explore) | Tags: military, eligibility, official. Tip: Armed Forces entry rules, fitness, and medical standards must come from official sources. MatchKite is guidance only, not recruitment advice. Do: Read the official pathway page. Write questions about fitness, quals, and daily life. Take those questions to an official information contact or event. Do not claim: Do not invent medical, fitness, pay, or posting facts.

HOWTO: follow_up | Source: National Careers Service — applications | Tags: follow_up, plan, backup. Tip: After you apply or enquire, note the date and keep one backup option warm so you are not waiting on a single reply. Do: Set a calendar reminder to check for a reply. Keep one other employer or course from your matches as a backup step. Do not claim: Do not assume silence means rejection or acceptance.

HOWTO: skills_proof | Source: Prospects — CVs and cover letters | Tags: skills, portfolio, evidence. Tip: Employers and tutors respond better when you can show skills with a short proof piece, not only a list of words. Do: Pick one proof that fits this match: a project photo, short write-up, GitHub link, club role, or care example. Add it to your CV or application notes. Do not claim: Do not fake portfolio work.
